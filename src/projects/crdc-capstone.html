01 / Context

The project.

A QUT student team capstone partnering with the Cotton Research and Development Corporation. The aim is to help researchers work across historical scientific reports while retaining evidence provenance.

The public demo is a static website published on ChatGPT Sites. The original Python retrieval and multi-agent pipeline is a separate application.

02 / Problem

What needed solving.

A relevant document can still contain chunks that do not answer the analytical task. Giving every specialist the same retrieval query can produce fluent synthesis that overlooks context, provenance or older evidence.

03 / My contribution

My part in the work.

In a QUT student team partnering with the Cotton Research and Development Corporation, my emphasis was lead-agent orchestration, task-specific retrieval, document/chunk evaluation, cross-encoder reranking, provenance and temporal diversity. The wider capstone is team work. This was not employment or an internship with CRDC.

04 / Architecture & implementation

How the pieces work.

Separate the research question from the analytical task and retrieval intent. A lead agent coordinates task-specific retrieval, ranking and evidence selection before synthesis. Keep source references attached to the evidence and inspect usefulness at both document and chunk level.

05 / Engineering trade-offs

Why these choices.

  1. Plan retrieval per analytical task

    Different specialists receive task-specific retrieval intent instead of the same broad query. This adds orchestration complexity but makes retrieval behaviour easier to inspect.

  2. Check evidence at two levels

    Evaluate document relevance and the actual chunks supplied to synthesis. Passage-level inspection takes more work than a document-only score, but exposes unsupported context.

  3. Preserve provenance and time context

    Keep source references attached to selected evidence and examine temporal diversity. Diversification can trade top-ranked similarity for a broader historical view; it needs task-level evaluation.

07 / Limitations

Limits and next steps.

The public account cannot independently establish retrieval quality. Within an approved evaluation, I would compare fixed question sets, inspect citation support and track how evidence selection changes across time periods. Partner review would be required before publishing any detailed findings.
